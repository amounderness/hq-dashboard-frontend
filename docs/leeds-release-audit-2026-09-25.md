# Leeds release audit · 25 September 2026

This audit applies to the local `leeds-local-elections-v0.1.0` development package. It does **not** authorize publication of that package. Its `publication_allowed` flag remains false. The separately built `leeds-public-results-v0.2.1` release uses only the audited public results, fresh ONS geometry and an aggregate retrospective Forecast test summary; it excludes census, Electoral Tribe and prospective Forecast data.

| Component | Evidence found | Release decision |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduled results, 2021–2024 | The source notebooks name the House of Commons Library Local Elections Handbook workbooks. The [2021](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/data/parliament-elections-data/2021-local-elections-handbook-and-dataset/), [2022](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/data/parliament-elections-data/2022-local-elections-handbook-and-dataset/), [2023](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/2023-local-elections-handbook-and-dataset/) and [2024](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/2024-local-elections-handbook-and-dataset/) pages expose downloads with the same filenames as the four local raw workbooks. The Commons Library [permits reuse of its published data under the Open Parliament Licence](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/data/). The earlier isolated rebuild reproduced the transformed result tables from the local files. Independent comparison with the [Leeds City Council archive](https://datamillnorth.org/dataset/local-election-results-20jwj) found 696 of 711 packaged candidate ward/vote pairs identical: 183/184 in 2021, 167/167 in 2022, 163/175 in 2023 and 183/185 in 2024. The 15 exceptions are detailed below and in `validation/official-history-crosscheck.json`. | The release uses the Handbook values in the 15 disputed rows because the original [2021](https://www.electionscentre.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/LEH2021-complete.pdf), [2023](https://www.electionscentre.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/LEH-2023-Complete.pdf) and [2024](https://www.electionscentre.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/LEH2024-Complete.pdf) Handbook PDFs corroborate them. The council archive discrepancies remain disclosed. Exact byte comparison of the four local Commons workbooks with fresh upstream downloads remains outstanding; do not describe them as hash-verified against the website. |
| 2026 council results; Morley South 2025 | The [Leeds City Council archive](https://datamillnorth.org/dataset/local-election-results-20jwj) lists both official files under OGL v3. The 2026 candidate records were checked against the council results page; the Morley South declaration was checked from the rendered PDF. | Source and licence are recorded in `sources.json`; retain direct links and attribution. |
| Farnley & Wortley, 10 October 2024 | The archive PDF has zeroes for all candidate votes and no winner. The package rejected it. | Keep as an explicitly missing event. A secondary report cannot silently replace a declaration. |
| 2025 ward map | The ONS [BFC V2 product](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/612b175b-987c-4acf-8ca4-1f93c6947928/wards-may-2025-boundaries-uk-bfc-v2) has the same internal layer name as the local GeoPackage, despite its BGC filename. An official ArcGIS query returned 33 Leeds features with exactly the package's 33 ward codes. All compared shapes were valid; maximum Hausdorff distance was 0.0000183 degrees and maximum symmetric-difference area was 0.374% of an official shape. [ONS boundary guidance](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/geographicalproducts/digitalboundaries) states the OGL and required ONS/OS credits. | Strong source match, but not an exact geometry match. Rebuild the web layer from the official BFC V2 service for a new release and include both required credits. Continue labelling all displayed years as *2025 boundaries*. |
| Census population reference | The local OA-to-ward lookup and ward atlas have hashes and reproduced aggregates, but exact upstream files/licence links are not recorded. | Exclude this layer from the first hosted factual release, or rebuild it from linked official ONS sources. |
| By-election coverage | The council archive lists Farnley 2024 and Morley South 2025, but no exhaustive register was certified. | Describe the exact included event set. Do not claim a complete latest-results history or current councillor composition. |
| Forecast | The first time-ordered backtest is in `forecast-research/`. Its 2026 holdout is materially worse than earlier folds. | Research only; no prospective ward, seat or council-control projection published. |

The four local raw Handbook workbook SHA-256 values are `b80b10d51856b82623cdaef48f33bc68a890fb2474d73a34f4e7affd5586850e` (2021), `0848b583f44aa277826d67b71a1b164cbd4e71cd658cea253e69441d26745681` (2022), `5e1861b842bb818ba8791faa22eb82b153f7a5ea045f9a9bd1e673d958579e2c` (2023) and `90b076b0cd542fceeea6b1c1c420ec51581262fed5d9e4dc5c7e064f625f67b1` (2024). These identify the local copies; they do not establish equality with fresh downloads.

The council comparison uses original archive files downloaded on 25 September 2026, hashed in `validation/official-history-crosscheck.json`. It compares the multiset of ward code and candidate votes, so equal vote totals for different candidates could theoretically conceal a name or party mismatch; it is not a candidate-identity certification. The 2024 workbook's West Yorkshire mayoral rows are excluded from this local-council comparison.

The exceptions are concrete. In **2021 Kirkstall**, the council CSV records Reiss Smith (known as Capitano), Conservative, on **733** votes; the Commons-derived package records Capitano R. on **773**, also printed in the 2021 Handbook PDF. In **2023 Calverley & Farsley** (five candidates) and **Headingley & Hyde Park** (seven), the council's published spreadsheet gives every candidate **zero**, while the Commons workbook and 2023 Handbook PDF contain the non-zero package totals. In **2024 Roundhay**, the council spreadsheet has Lisa Martin **4,039** and Shazar Ahad **839**, each one below the Commons workbook, 2024 Handbook PDF and package (**4,040** and **840**). These look like council source-file errors, but that remains an inference. The release follows the Handbook and explicitly discloses the differences. The council files are [2021](https://datamillnorth.org/download/20jwj/773088bc-22fd-496b-9362-82235b230ced/Results_06May2021.CSV), [2022](https://datamillnorth.org/download/20jwj/3d356930-2678-44d8-8d64-cc339e051aa3/Results_05May2022.csv), [2023](https://datamillnorth.org/download/20jwj/fd9656bf-6ffe-4148-93da-a14ac48a07cd/Results%20for%20Data%20Mill%20North.xlsx) and [2024](https://datamillnorth.org/download/20jwj/e3x/Results%20from%20LCC%20%26%20WYCA%20Elections%20held%20on%202%20May%202024.xlsx).

The official ONS Leeds query used `WD_MAY_2025_UK_BFC_V2/FeatureServer/0`, `LAD25CD='E08000035'`, and EPSG:4326. The downloaded response is retained in the local audit workspace, not in Git. A release rebuild must record its retrieval date, URL, response hash and geometry validation.
